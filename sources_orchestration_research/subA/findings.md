- **Architecture** (layers, components, control loops)
  - The article describes the **ETSI ZSM reference architecture** (cited as ref [10] = *ETSI GS ZSM 002, "Zero-touch Network and Service Management (ZSM); Reference Architecture", Aug. 2019*), not an architecture of its own.
  - Building blocks (verbatim): *"the framework architecture is composed of a set of architectural building blocks, namely, management domains (MDs) including E2E service MD, management services, integration fabric and common data services."* Plus a **Digital Storefront** ("Automated Customer & Business Management").
  - **Management domains (MDs):** *"The ZSM architecture is split into MDs to support the separation of management concerns. Each MD is responsible for intelligent automation of orchestration, control and assurance of resources and services within its scope."* The **E2E service MD** is *"a special MD that manages end-to-end, customer-facing services that span multiple domains provided by different administrative entities."*
  - **Logical groups of management services inside an MD** (verbatim): *"domain collection services, domain analytics services, domain intelligence services, domain orchestration services and domain control services."*
  - **Integration fabric:** intra-domain integration fabric for local service exposure/consumption, and **inter-domain integration fabric** for cross-domain exposure; *"The management services are exposed and consumed following either the request-response or the publish-subscribe patterns."*
  - **Common Data Services:** *"allow to separate data storage and data processing, facilitating access to data and cross-domain data exposure."*
  - **Control loops:** *"Data in Common Data Services can be exploited by domain and E2E service intelligence services to drive domain-level and cross-domain AI-based closed-loop automation, respectively. The automated decision-making mechanisms are controlled by rules and policies."*
  - Design principles (verbatim): *"modular (made up of self-contained and loosely-coupled services), extensible …, scalable … and resilient to failures"*, *"paired with the use of intent-based interfaces, closed-loop operation and AI/ML techniques to empower full-automation of the management operations."*
  - **Fig. 2 (Management Domain's High-Level Architecture)** shows the loop: `Domain Data Collection → Domain Data Services / Data Store → Domain Analytics → (Anomaly detection) → Domain Intelligence → Domain Orchestration → "Preconfigured or dynamically created workflow" → Domain Control → "Change configuration" / "Control the state of managed entities" → managed entities.`

- **Main limitations** (as stated + methodological)
  - **As stated by the authors**, the limitations of AI-driven ZSM are: (1) **"Lack of Datasets and Labeling"** — *"5G-specific datasets are crucial … Unfortunately, such datasets are actually scarce since the roll-out of 5G networks is planned for 2020. Moreover, the existing operators' data are not accessible due to privacy issues"*, and even recent initiatives' samples *"are synthetic and/or lack completeness"*; (2) **"AI Model Interpretability"** — black-box deep/ensemble models, and *"a tradeoff between interpretability and accuracy should be established"*; (3) **"Training Time and Inference Accuracy"** — *"the lengthy training time required to achieve such improved accuracy may jeopardize their practicality for real-time usage"*; (4) **"Computation Complexity"** — the accuracy gain *"comes at the cost of high demand of computation, memory and energy resources"*; (5) **Security** — a *"source for new attack vectors"*: *"ML techniques are vulnerable to several attacks [12] targeting both training phase (i.e., poisoning attacks) and test phase (i.e., evasion attacks)"*; and (6) **GDPR/accountability** — *"the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) law entitles individuals the right to obtain an explanation of how the decision is reached by an automated system."*
  - **Methodological limitations I observe:** (a) the paper is **architectural/positional** — no ZSM prototype, no orchestrator, no deployment, therefore **no evidence that the surveyed AI techniques compose into a working zero-touch system**; (b) the single experiment is on **intrusion-detection classification (CICIDS2017, DoS/DDoS only)**, which is *adjacent* to, but not, a network-management-orchestration workload — the results do not transfer directly to orchestration decisions such as VNF placement or slice scaling; (c) the benchmark compares only **DLP/CNN-free MLP and LSTM variants** on one dataset, so *"up to 7.5×/4.3×"* is conditional on that dataset and hardware pair; (d) the security discussion is largely **illustrative** (Fig. 3 is described as an *"Adversarial Attack Illustrative Example"*), with no attack implemented or measured.

- **Research gap this suggests**
  - The article's own "Future Research Directions" are the gap list, verbatim headings: **(A) "Safe Shared Learning"** — *"It is necessary to ensure that the model can learn from shared data without compromising the privacy of collaborative entities"*; **(B) "Trust in Data and Models"** — including *"solutions to automatically collect trusted immutable datasets from distributed sources"*, with **blockchain** floated as an enabler; **(C) "Computation Complexity Optimization"** — *"new optimization techniques should be designed to reduce the complexity of those models without loss of accuracy"*; **(D) "Training Speed – Accuracy Balance"**, with **transfer learning** named as promising but *"a major challenge for applying transfer learning paradigm is to identify what, how and when to transfer knowledge"*; **(E) "Adversarial ML for ZSM"** — *"there are no clues on how they could be crafted and introduced in a network traffic. Research work should focus on devising algorithms that automatically generate adversarial examples for network traffic"*, plus *"a certification framework to assess the security properties of ML techniques"*; **(F) "Learning Correctly in the Presence of Adversaries."**
  - **Gap for a net-orchestration review:** there is a clear, explicitly stated absence of (i) **real 5G datasets and testbeds** for AI-driven management, (ii) **interpretable/trustworthy** management models, and (iii) **adversarially robust** closed-loop orchestration — i.e., the zero-touch loop is described normatively, and its *robustness and verifiability* are open problems. A follow-up work would need to demonstrate a real closed-loop ZSM/ZSM-compliant system with SLA/security guarantees, not just an architecture plus a classifier benchmark.

- **Type classification — pick EXACTLY ONE and justify in one sentence:**
  - **(D) an orchestration architecture/framework design.**
  - *Justification:* the paper's core contribution is a **novel hierarchical, decentralized zero-touch management-and-orchestration framework** — the **IDMO / DMO / ISM** three-tier decomposition with **hierarchical closed-control loops (MS, AE, DE)** mapped onto ETSI ZSM/ENI and instantiated for the **cloud (NFV MANO)** and **RAN (O-RAN)** domains — and it *"proposes"* and *"devises"* that framework rather than shipping a running orchestrator (type A) or contributing a standalone allocation algorithm (type B). - **Architecture** (layers, components, control loops)
  - **Three management tiers** (verbatim definitions):
    1. **IDMO — Inter-Domain Management and Orchestration**: *"IDMO is a centralized component with full-scope slice management and orchestration decision capabilities. It may take global actions for network-wide, cross-slice, and cross-domain optimizations."* Also: *"IDMO is equivalent to the 3GPP Network Slice Management Function (NSMF) [10] and exposes the Northbound API (NBI) for the OSS/BSS or Consumer Service Management Function (CSMF). IDMO is in charge of the LCM of end-to-end network slices."*
    2. **DMO — Domain Management Orchestrator**: *"Each technological domain is managed and orchestrated by its own entity, Domain Management Orchestrator (DMO), which is equivalent in 3GPP to the Network Sub-Slice Management Function (NSSMF)"*; *"Depending on the technological domain, a NSSMF may correspond to NFVO for Cloud/Edge, RANO for RAN, and Software Defined Networking (SDN) controller for the case of the transport network."*
    3. **ISM — InSlice-Manager**: *"The Inslice-Manager is the finest management entity that handles fault management, configuration management, and performance management of the service deployed on top of the sub-slice."* *"ISM can be considered as a slice-level Element Manager (EM), with interfaces to the EMs of the slice's VNFs and PNF."* Two flavours: **ISM of a tenant-specific sub-slice** and **ISM of a shared sub-slice** (operator-managed).
  - **Closed-control loop components** (verbatim): *"Figure 2 shows the components of the Closed-control loop: Monitoring System (MS), Analytical Engine (AE), and Decision Engine (DE)."*

- **Research gap this suggests**
  - **The single biggest gap this survey exposes for a net-orchestration review:** as of this COMST survey, VNF/VM placement research was **algorithm-centric, single-cloud, offline-dataset-driven, and disconnected from the operational control planes** (no ZSM, no ONAP/OSM, no O-RAN, no cloud-native/Kubernetes placement). Placement was studied as a **static or periodically-replanned optimization problem**, not as a **closed-loop, SLA-governed orchestration function**.
  - Concretely, the survey's own challenges imply the following open problems: **(i)** placement at **large-scale, multi-tenant** cloud setups (explicitly *"unresolved"*, and reviewed solutions' constraints *"don't consider the case of multi-tenant cloud environments"*); **(ii)** **multi-cloud / federated-cloud** and cross-domain placement (only *"a few"* works; one reviewed solution *"Cannot know if it will be performing well in a federated environment"*); **(iii)** **reliability and k-resiliency**-aware placement; **(iv)** **online placement overhead** and **data-transfer-time** optimization; **(v)** under-studied objectives — **overhead, ROI, aggregate traffic**; **(vi)** under-served use cases — **vCPE/SD-WAN, mobile base-station virtualization, CDN virtualization, VPaaS**; **(vii)** **container placement** (explicitly deferred: *"there are not many solutions for container placement in the literature"*), and by extension the whole cloud-native/Kubernetes placement problem; **(viii)** **realistic evaluation environments** bridging simulation and real deployments, with SLA and UE-consumption realism; and **(ix)** preserving **management context** in service-agnostic virtual equipment. Exact-CSP approaches *"cannot be applied to very large datasets"*, and most reviewed work is **offline**: *"most of the work mentioned in the literature is dedicated to offline VMP, except in [100]."*
  - For an orchestrator-focused review, this survey is therefore best cited as evidence that **the placement literature it surveys is *not* orchestrator work** — it supplies type-(B) optimization algorithms that an orchestrator *could* consume, which is exactly the (A)-vs-(B) distinction the parent's taxonomy needs.
